## The attacks, and what held

- **Within 64 values** (EXECUTED, 6 MiB frames, `output_bytes` 1 MiB and None, on the first build): numbers of six million digits (float, exponent, integer), a key of six million characters, 32 duplicate long keys, six million bytes of whitespace, 60 levels of objects around a long string, escapes of every kind, a long `$bytes` value: each between 1.0 and 3.75 times the frame above the frame itself. The worst was one long string with one four-byte character (L1), which the fix answers before decoding.
- **The count and the text inside strings** hold on bytes: the token pattern takes `\` and the next byte as one pair, so `\"` and `\\` never flip the state; no byte of a UTF-8 character past ASCII is a token. A desync needs a backslash outside a string, where `json.loads` stops at the same byte, and the fix counts nothing there (L2). The text sum is a lower bound on the strings' decoded UTF-8: a raw byte is itself, an escape is counted one, and every escape decodes to at least one byte (READ); a display that fits holds at most `(4 × room + 8) // 3 + ENVELOPE` of it, base64 being at most `(4n + 8) / 3` for n bytes (READ). EXECUTED: none of 3,000 random guest-shaped displays that fit their room (PNG, control characters, quotes, two-, three- and four-byte characters) was refused or passed the bound; the unit row keeps a PNG of `room - 1` bytes.
- **Under tools** a frame of more than 64 values goes to the tool host, whose `__call__` runs its own `admit` (its `_pending` is None, since the result host never calls it) and refuses one past 1 MiB unread (READ, `_tool_host.py`; EXECUTED, the unit row). A frame within the tool cap is parsed whatever its text, since its `op` is not known unparsed (L5).
- **The residual** (EXECUTED, the fix, room R = 1 MiB, frames of 6R): escapes `A`, `\u0001`, `一` or surrogate pairs whose text fits, with one raw four-byte character, held 26 to 28 times R above the frame; with a three-byte character instead, 14; spread over six MIME types or 60 keys, 21 and 24. With the frame and its second copy at the door, the builder's 35.4 times `output_bytes` is the ceiling I reach too. It comes from CPython holding the whole frame's text four bytes a character once one character needs four: a constant of the string model, reached only through a frame six times the room, bounded by `admit`'s length rule, linear in `output_bytes` and the same for every frame shape.
- **`output_bytes=None`** stays unbounded in length and costs at most about nine times the frame when it is read; `None` already lifts the bound on stdout and stderr, so it opens no new class. Decision 4 states it.

I find no simple sound rule the builder missed: counting `\u` escapes against the room is what the fix does; a cheaper decode does not exist for `json.loads`, which takes one `str` whose width its widest character sets; refusing `\u` escapes of printable characters would refuse a sender that writes ASCII-only JSON, which the protocol allows. I do not ask for it for this release; the owner may reserve it.
