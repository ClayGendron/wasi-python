## Short version

The sandbox holds. Every a6 probe that could be re-run gives the same answer on a7: the host filesystem, the slash escapes through all three linkers, the mounts and their limits, the leftovers, the door's cancel, matplotlib's read-only data grant and the errno mapping. After normalising random names, paths and timings, the only differences are a scratch path and two timings (`logs/`).

**The open-file cap (068) holds.** On default arguments a guest that opens files in a loop now stops at 1,024 with `EMFILE`, in the zip, the root, a grant, folders and `scandir` alike, and the host's own count rises by 1,032 at most. In a6 the same guest held 122,869 descriptors and the embedder's thread got `EMFILE`. A session counts across turns and its ending.

**Sessions end inside their budget.** Twelve hostile `atexit` handlers and finalizers (a spin, `sleep(1e6)`, `select`, a handler that catches everything, a looping `__del__`, deep recursion, a deep free that traps, files to the cap, an attempt at 100 GiB of output, `os._exit`, 3 GiB of memory) were each closed, expired idle, and dropped unclosed. Every ending finished or was stopped within 1.1 s of its 1 s budget. Afterwards no extra threads, descriptors or open sessions were left.

**The recursion guard (071) holds on this host.** Fourteen recursion shapes ran on the main thread and on threads of 16 MiB, 1 MiB and 768 KiB. Several of them made host calls at the deepest frame. Each ended with `RecursionError`, a clean `stack overflow` trap or the deadline, and the host process lived through all 56 runs. The one known way a guest can kill the host remains musl's 128 KiB threads (documented; spec 078 reserved; see N2).

**The release path ties what it signs to what it built.** The helper traced every hand-off. `gather` reads 19 records by id and SHA-256 and has no `if:`. `attest`, `publish` and `pypi` each re-check the assets against the build's hash, and need a pushed tag. The dry run 39240833359 on `08de0c8` passed 26 jobs and skipped the three that publish. The helper found three low gaps in what `gather` checks (F3 to F5).

**The supply chain is clean.** There are 4 new pins. Each is re-hashed at use and equals upstream's published hash. The 2 new patches are hashed in `MANIFEST` and touch no parser, bound or access rule. The four new licence files are byte for byte upstream's. a6's F2 (the unpinned host `python3`) is closed by spec 079, and a6's F1 (an async gate's `GeneratorExit`) by spec 075. I re-ran the F1 probe: all 18 rows now fail closed.

**lxml and regex reach nothing outside the grants.** No XXE, XInclude or XSLT read or write left a grant; a host listener counted no connection; billion laughs stop at libxml2's amplification limit; 14,000 fuzzed documents and 9,900 encoding cases gave no trap; a runaway regex stops at the deadline, the fuel cap or a cancel.

**New findings.** The worst (F1, medium, not new in a7): a guest given `root=True` can leave its private root on the host's disk after the call, by building a tree deeper than the process's free descriptors, so its disk use adds up across calls. Two README claims are false (F2, F3): `XSLTAccessControl.DENY_ALL` does not stop `xsl:import` or `xsl:include`, and regex's `timeout=` never fires above about 2.147 s. The deep-recursion list misses some traps (F4). `gather` has three low gaps in what it checks (F5 to F7).

### The open-file cap (EXECUTED, `probes/p23_descriptors.py`, `p30_open_file_cap.py`, `p30b_renumber_session.py`)

The probe raised its soft limit to 200,000 first, so that only the cap could stop the guest. A host thread counted `/dev/fd` while the guest held what it got.

| guest | stopped at | host descriptors, peak over base | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| a6's `p23`: the data grant's `matplotlibrc` in a loop, default arguments | 1,024, `EMFILE` (33) | +1,032 | back to base |
| the zip in a loop | 1,024, `EMFILE` | +1,032 | back to base |
| 300 folders in the zip, 300 files in the root, 300 folders in a grant, then `scandir` | 1,024 in all, `EMFILE` | +1,031 | back to base |
| `max_open_files=10` | 10 | +13 | back to base |
| `max_open_files=0` | 0 (the stdlib's own opens refused too, as documented) | +3 | back to base |
| a session, cap 100, 60 opens per turn over three turns | 100 in turn 2, and again in turn 3 | +106 | back to base after close |
| that session's ending opening in a loop | inside the same 100 | — | `finished` |

- **The embedder kept working.** In a6, while the guest held its descriptors, the embedder's own `open` failed with `EMFILE`. In a7 it succeeded throughout.
- **`fd_renumber` is not reachable from Python.** `os.dup` answers 58 (ENOTSUP), and the guest has no `os.dup2` and no `fcntl`. So only compiled guest code could renumber. I read the rule (`mounts.py`, `_fd_renumber`): a renumber discards `to` from the held set and moves `from` onto it. That never under-counts the descriptors the guest holds (READ).
- **What the cap does not count** is the documented gap, spec 072: the descriptors wasmtime holds while it resolves a deep path. Measured (`p31_deep_path.py`): a guest with a writable root built 600 levels in 20 s (each `mkdir` resolves the whole path). Then each `stat` of the deepest path held 613 host descriptors while it ran. The tree's depth is bounded by time, and by the process's own limit: past it, the guest's `mkdir` gets EIO (`p31c`). So under macOS's default soft limit of 256 a guest can still bring the process to its limit, as *Not protected* says. Not a new finding.

### Sessions end inside their budget (EXECUTED, `probes/p32_session_ending.py`, `logs/p32_session_ending.log`)

Each case opened a session with `shutdown_s=1` and `max_open_files=200`. One turn registered the hostile code. The session was then closed, left to expire idle (`idle_s=0.5`), or dropped with no `close()`.

| hostile ending | close | idle | dropped |
|---|---|---|---|
| a spin; `sleep(1e6)`; `select` for 1e6 s | stopped, 1.02 s | stopped, 1.5–1.65 s | stopped, 1.02–1.08 s |
| a handler that catches every exception around a spin | stopped, 1.01 s | stopped | stopped |
| a looping `__del__` at finalization | stopped, 1.01 s | stopped | stopped |
| unbounded recursion; a deep free that traps | stopped, 1.09 s | stopped | stopped |
| open files to the cap, then spin | stopped, 1.02 s | stopped | stopped |
| an attempt at 100 GiB of output | stopped, 1.02 s (the ending's output is not kept) | stopped | stopped |
| a handler that re-registers itself | finished, 0.11 s | finished | finished |
| `os._exit(7)`; a 3 GiB allocation | finished, at once | finished | finished |

- The idle times count from the end of the turn, so they include the 0.5 s bound.
- After all 36 endings: two more threads than at the start (the reaper, and one that stays), the same through every case. Five more descriptors, also constant. `sessions_open` was 0.
- The guest cannot catch the stop: it is an epoch trap, not an exception (READ, `_shutdown`). `KeyboardInterrupt` is not used.
- **READ, the rest of the ending.** `_shutdown_applies` runs Python's shutdown only after a turn that returned, so never on an interpreter that a trap or a stop unwound. The guard against replaced signal handlers is checked before the shutdown, and before every turn and opening. `_ending_seconds` caps the ending at the opening plus the lifetime plus `shutdown_s`. The exit hook waits on each session's ending with no timeout. That wait is bounded by the budget, and by any embedder host function, tool or provider the ending calls. That is the embedder's own code, as the docstring says.

### Deep recursion and the host process (EXECUTED, `probes/p33_recursion.py`, `p33_recursion_child.py`, `p33b_recursion_limit.py`)

Each stack size ran in its own child process, so that a crash would show as a return code.

- **The cases:** plain recursion with the limit raised to 10^6; recursion that opens a file, sleeps or calls a tool at its deepest frame (that is, through the runner's Python host functions, which 068 now puts on every call's `path_open`); `repr(self)`; deep nested-list `repr`, `==` and free; `json.loads('['*10**6)`; `pickle`; deep `compile`; nested expat callbacks; a deep `re` pattern; a numpy `frompyfunc` callback.
- **The thread sizes:** the main thread, and threads of 16 MiB, 1 MiB and 768 KiB.
- **Results:**
  - every child lived to its end, with return code 0, in all 56 runs;
  - `repr(self)`, the `re` pattern and the numpy callback raised `RecursionError`;
  - the others ended with the trap `stack overflow`, as the README lists;
  - the depth cases with host calls ended at the 60 s deadline: unwinding a million Python frames, each making one host call, takes longer than that.
- **One more trap the README does not list** (`p33b`). Plain Python-to-Python recursion raises `RecursionError` at 997 levels with the default limit. With the guest's own `sys.setrecursionlimit(3000)` or more, it traps instead, on both binaries. This is inside the guest, but it belongs in the list (F4).
- **The signal guard** (READ, `_trap_handlers_ready`) is Linux-only, by wasmtime's mechanism. macOS uses Mach ports and Windows a vectored handler. I could not exercise it here. 071's build review ran it on glibc and musl, and the release's Linux shards run its checks.
- **musl** is the known way left for a guest to kill the host: N2.

## Findings, most severe first

### F1. A guest given `root=True` can make its private root outlive the call. Medium; does not block a7; fix before 0.1.0 final (EXECUTED)

The README's *Safety* list and *Not protected* say that a private root, with its lock file, stays behind only when the process is killed. Any other call removes it when it ends. That does not hold against a hostile guest.

**What I ran** (`probes/p31d_private_root_dirfd.py`, `logs/p31d_private_root_dirfd.log`). The guest builds a deep tree one level at a time through `dir_fd`. It holds one descriptor at a time, so it is never refused:

```python
fd = os.open("/", os.O_RDONLY | os.O_DIRECTORY)
for i in range(D):
    os.mkdir("a", dir_fd=fd)
    n = os.open("a", os.O_RDONLY | os.O_DIRECTORY, dir_fd=fd); os.close(fd); fd = n
```

| process soft limit | depth | the call | private root and lock after `run` returned |
|---|---|---|---|
| 256 (macOS's default) | 400 | exit 0 | **both left behind** |
| 1,024 (a common Linux default) | 1,500 | exit 0 | **both left behind** |

- `run` raised nothing and returned exit 0. The next call worked.
- The same tree built with whole-path `mkdir` stops at the process's limit with EIO, and is then removed (`p31c`). The `dir_fd` route is what gets past it.

**Why** (READ, `runner.py`, `_Private.remove`). The removal is `shutil.rmtree(self.path, ignore_errors=True)`. Python's descriptor-safe `rmtree` keeps one descriptor open per level while it descends. So it fails, silently, on a tree deeper than the process's free descriptors. Then the lock file is kept, since the folder still exists, and the name is dropped from `_held`. Nothing in this process retries. The next process's sweep tries once, at its first call, with the same `rmtree`, and fails the same way under the same limit. A session's private root, its scratch folder and a fresh `HOME` take the same path (INFERRED, same code).

**What it breaks:**
- **Disk.** *Not protected* bounds a guest's disk use by "its deadline", because the private root goes when the call ends. A guest that leaves its tree, with any data under it, makes the use add up across calls. A long-running server that gives untrusted code `root=True` can be filled call by call.
- **The promise.** The README promises the removal, and the call reports nothing when it does not happen.
- **What it does not break.** The folder is private (`0700`) and never reused by a later call. So there is no cross-run contamination, and nothing outside the temporary folder changes.

**Not new in a7** (INFERRED): `_Private.remove` and the guest's `dir_fd` calls were the same in a6. It is found now because 068's work drew attention to descriptors.

**Fix** (host side, `runner.py` only, in the wheel; no rebuild):
- Remove trees without holding a descriptor per level. One way: walk down by `openat` holding two descriptors. At each folder, rename its subfolders up into the private root's top level (`os.rename(name, top_name, src_dir_fd=…, dst_dir_fd=…)` within the one filesystem), so the tree becomes wide instead of deep. Then remove each piece. Another way: catch `EMFILE` from `rmtree` and fall back to that.
- Keep the name in `_held` until the folder is really gone, and retry it at the next call's sweep.
- **Check:** a guest builds a tree deeper than the test process's soft limit through `dir_fd` under `root=True` (and in a session). Afterwards no `wasi-python-root-*` entry and no lock file from that call remains. Run it under a lowered `RLIMIT_NOFILE` in a subprocess, so the check counts entries and times nothing.

### F2. `XSLTAccessControl.DENY_ALL` does not stop `xsl:import` or `xsl:include`, and an imported stylesheet resolves external entities. Low; README only; correct before the tag (EXECUTED by the helper; log re-read by me)

- **The claim** (README, *lxml in the guest*, lines 256-264). It lists `xsl:include` and `xsl:import` among a stylesheet's loads. Then it says that with `DENY_ALL` "the read or write then raises".
- **What happens** (`xml-probes/x04b_deny_all_import.py`, `.log`). Under `DENY_ALL`, `<xsl:import href="/ro/ent.xsl"/>` compiles. `ent.xsl` declares `<!ENTITY s SYSTEM "/ro/data.txt">`, and the result holds `data.txt`'s content. The same holds for `xsl:include`. Compiling also tells a stylesheet whether a granted file exists.
- **Why** (helper, READ). lxml sets the access control on the transform context only (`xslt.pxi:636-637`), not around `xsltParseStylesheetDoc` (`xslt.pxi:403`). Native lxml does the same.
- **Who it reaches.** Not the guest as adversary: the guest can `open()` its grants anyway, and nothing left the grants. It reaches a guest program that transforms an untrusted stylesheet, which is exactly the case the README's advice is for. An embedder following the README is told they are covered when they are not.
- **Fix:** correct the paragraph. Say that `DENY_ALL` covers the transform, not the stylesheet's own `xsl:import` and `xsl:include`. Say to parse an untrusted stylesheet with a parser whose `etree.Resolver` refuses every URL; with that parser, both raise (helper, EXECUTED, `x04c_import_resolver_fix`). Add a check that holds both sentences.

### F3. regex's `timeout=` never fires above about 2.147 s. Low; README only; correct before the tag (EXECUTED, by the helper and by me)

- **The claim** (README lines 181-183): `timeout=` "counts the guest's clock, so whether a match times out depends on the host's speed".
- **What happens.** `timeout=0.5`, `1.5`, `2.0` and `2.1` fire on time. `2.2`, `3`, `5`, `1e9` and `1e30` never fire. `(x+x+)+y` ran 35 s under `timeout=5` (`xml-probes/x10d`, `x10a`; `probes/p35`).
- **Why** (helper, READ). wasi-libc's `CLOCKS_PER_SEC` is 10^9. regex keeps clock ticks in `Py_ssize_t`, which is 32 bits on wasm32. So `decode_timeout` overflows (`_regex.c:21092`), and `check_timed_out`'s difference wraps every 4.295 s (`_regex.c:2258`).
- **Who it reaches.** Inside the guest only: the embedder's fuel, deadline and cancel hold. But the README names the wrong cause, and a program that relies on `timeout=5` against a hostile pattern runs until the call's own limit.
- **Fix:** correct the sentence now. Later, carry a patch that keeps the ticks in `clock_t`, with its upstream draft (32-bit hosts have the same bug at about 2,147 s), and a check that `timeout=3` fires.
