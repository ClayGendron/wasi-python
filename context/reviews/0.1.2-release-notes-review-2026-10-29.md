## Findings, most severe first

Each rewrite below is the whole `- ` line, to replace the line it names in `CHANGELOG.md`'s 0.1.2 section, in the preparation pull request, as Decision 5 allows. Every one was checked with the section it lands in (see "The section with the rewrites applied").

1. **Medium M1: the Fixed `close()` entry names the wrong caller** (`CHANGELOG.md`, 0.1.2, Fixed, first entry). The deadlock was a `host=` function's or a mount provider's, which run on the turn's own thread. A `close()` from a tool never hung: a tool runs on a thread of its own, and its `close()` waits briefly for the turn to stop (READ, 144's landing notes, departure 1, "The deadlock is a host function's, not a tool's"; `docs/how-to/sessions.md:67`, the page the entry links, says the same). So the lead says a tool's `close()` was fixed, and the fact says a tool's `close()` "now returns at once", which the linked page contradicts. A reader whose `host=` callback hung will not recognise the fix; a reader who calls `close()` from a tool is told the wrong behaviour. Rewrite:

   ```
   - **`close()` in a host function no longer hangs; every session ends.** A `close()` from a `host=` function or a mount's provider during `run_async` used to deadlock; it now returns at once and the session ends with its turn. A turn cancelled while queued, or still queued at exit, no longer leaves its session open. [Know how a session ends](https://claygendron.github.io/wasi-python/how-to/sessions/#know-how-a-session-ends)
   ```

2. **Medium M2: the advisories entry says more than the security policy does** (`CHANGELOG.md`, 0.1.2, Security, last entry). The entry says "None of them reaches the wasmtime this release runs, 50.0.0". `SECURITY.md:30` and `docs/explanation/security-model.md`, "Three wasmtime fixes, in wasmtime itself", say something narrower: 50.0.0 is outside the critical GHSA-mghf-wjfp-p5xm's range, and the other four advisories are in `wasi-http` and the component model, "which the runner does not use". They do not say 50.0.0 is free of those four. A reader who drives wasmtime 50.0.0 for other work could take the note as a clean bill for the engine. The lead also says what the policy does, not what the reader gets. Rewrite:

   ```
   - **wasmtime's advisories of 2026-10-19 do not reach the runner.** wasmtime 50.0.0, which this release runs, is outside the critical [GHSA-mghf-wjfp-p5xm](https://github.com/bytecodealliance/wasmtime/security/advisories/GHSA-mghf-wjfp-p5xm)'s range, and the other four are in parts of wasmtime the runner does not use. [Three wasmtime fixes, in wasmtime itself](https://claygendron.github.io/wasi-python/explanation/security-model/#three-wasmtime-fixes-in-wasmtime-itself)
   ```
