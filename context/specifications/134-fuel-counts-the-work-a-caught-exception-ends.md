## The problem

`run()` calls fuel "a cap on the guest's executed instructions", and `SECURITY.md` lists a guest that runs past its fuel cap as in scope. On wasmtime-py 49.0.0, the only version the pin admits, that is not what fuel counts.

GHSA-m63x-6p34-q65x (medium, fixed in wasmtime 49.0.1): on the exceptional edge of a call inside `try_table`, Cranelift's fuel accounting resets fuel to what remained before the call, so the callee's work before the throw is not counted. The project's advisory memo of 2026-10-18 rated it "unlikely: our C-built core module uses neither [proposal]" and said so was INFERRED, not shown by disassembly. The audit disassembled: `python.wasm` is linked with `-fwasm-exceptions` (`scripts/steps/60_link.sh:129`), every C++ package is compiled with it (`scripts/steps/55_matplotlib.sh:50,102`; `recipes/wasi-scipy.cross:44` and the other cross files), and the binary carries **44,791 `try_table` sites in 7,905 functions and 2 tags** (`__cpp_exception`, `__c_longjmp`). Every caught C++ exception, and every `longjmp` under `-wasm-enable-sjlj`, takes the edge the advisory names.

**From guest Python** (EXECUTED twice, independently, through `wasi_python.run` on the shipped set with fuel given and `deadline_s=None`): kiwisolver's `Solver.addConstraint` catches `UnsatisfiableConstraint` after the C++ solver builds a row whose size grows with the system. One failing call is counted at about 17,000 to 19,600 fuel whether the chain has 200, 2,000 or 6,000 variables, while its wall time grows from 9.6 to 190 µs. A same-shape no-throw control is counted at 140,067 fuel per call at 200 variables and 1,552,404 at 2,000. Under a cap of 2.2e9, 20,000 failing calls on a 2,000-variable chain finished with exit 0 and 2.02e9 fuel used; the same-shape no-throw loop and a weak-strength add-and-remove loop each ended `out of fuel` under the same cap. A standalone WAT module shows the engine defect alone: 100 rounds of a 100,000-iteration callee that throws count as 1,101 fuel on wasmtime-py 49.0.0 and 80,001,285 on the pinned CLI 49.0.3 (`agents/v04-eh-fuel/scratch/evidence/eh_fuel.wat`). By wall-time calibration the under-count is about 100 times at 6,000 variables.

**What still holds.** A finite `deadline_s` (the default is 60 s) bounds wall time through epoch interruption, which the advisory does not touch. So the cap is lost only where fuel is the bound an embedder relies on: `deadline_s=None` with fuel, a fuel cap tighter than the deadline, or `fuel_used` read as an instruction count. The security model's "a guest that waits with `deadline_s=None`" bullet is the nearest existing sentence; it does not say this.

**Two more facts of the binaries the memo got wrong or left unmeasured** (h17, h18, h12, EXECUTED): GHSA-p79w-pv2r-29qp is avoided only because the tag count is even (2); one more tag would meet its precondition on 49.0.0 to 49.0.2. GHSA-hw8m-q44c-ggrf is unreached not because "no GC proposal" but because no `exnref` local is live into a catch continuation across 102,646 `try_call` edges, a property of this LLVM 23 / wasi-sdk 34 build that another toolchain could change. The `br_table` maximum of 3,203 labels (against about 256k for GHSA-mghf-wjfp-p5xm) is measured by hand.

## Decisions

### 1. The check: a failing call's fuel grows with the system as the no-throw call's does

One slow-tier check, counted in fuel only (AGENTS.md: tests count operations, never wall-clock), `tests/checks/test_v1_4_embedding.py::test_fuel_counts_the_work_a_caught_exception_ends` or its own file in the `own-embedding` shard: the kiwisolver chain at two sizes (200 and 2,000 variables), 50 failing `addConstraint` calls and 50 same-shape no-throw add-and-remove calls per size, each kind in its own `run` call with `fuel=` far above the work, beside a run of the same chain with no calls. Per-call fuel is (the run with 50 calls − the same chain's run with none) / 50, on the plain reactor; its baseline repeats within 23 fuel (EXECUTED, the pre-build review), so 50 calls put the signal far above the noise. `deadline_s=600`, a backstop only (so the check cannot hang on a slow runner). The assertion is a **ratio**, not a number: the failing call's per-call fuel at 2,000 variables is at least 3 times its fuel at 200, as the no-throw call's is (about 11 times, EXECUTED). On 49.0.0 the failing call's fuel is flat (18,619 against 18,112, the audit) and the row is red. The pre-build review measured the same chain and calls by hand, 200 calls a run: the failing call 18,674 fuel at 200 variables and 17,805 at 2,000 on wasmtime-py 49.0.0 (ratio 0.95), and 97,529 and 925,098 on wasmtime-py 50.0.1 (ratio 9.5); the no-throw call 150,314 and 1,550,666 on both, to the instruction (ratio 10.3) (EXECUTED, `../reviews/134-fuel-counts-the-work-a-caught-exception-ends-spec-2026-10-25.md`). So the bound of 3 is red on 49.0.0 and green on 50 with margin on both sides. Its guest program is the audit's driver, rewritten to the judge's conventions (`agents/v05-eh-fuel-recheck/scratch/`).

**The gap.** Until 127 lands, the row enters `tests/known_gaps.toml` with its closer named. The row reports through `scan.report` with findings in words and no numbers (the fuel pairs go to the log line, not the finding), one finding per size pair that fails the bound, e.g. `a failing call's fuel at 2,000 variables is under 3 times its fuel at 200`. Its entry: `check = "tests/checks/test_v1_4_embedding.py::test_fuel_counts_the_work_a_caught_exception_ends"` (the node id of the file the builder chooses), `match = "1 finding(s), digest <the digest the red run prints>"`, `reason = "fuel under-counts C++ work that ends in a caught exception on wasmtime 49.0.0, fixed upstream in 49.0.1"`, `closes_with = "context/specifications/127-the-pin-moves-to-wasmtime-50.md"`. A deadline trap or any other failure prints a different digest, so it is red, never the gap. The gap is written by this specification, never by a builder turning a run green (rule 4). When 127 lands after 134, 127's builder deletes the gap entry and the row is green; 127's Decision 9 and claim list name the deletion.

### 2. The binaries' facts: one per-build row, two research facts

One new row beside the artifact-set checks (`tests/checks/test_v1_1_artifact_set.py`), reading each binary with the harness's own reader (`tests/harness/wasm.py`), green today, a fact that another toolchain or another package could move. Each new row is `@BINARY`, over the plain and the snapshot binary.

- **the tag section holds an even count** of tags (2 today), with the comment that an odd count meets GHSA-p79w's precondition on wasmtime below 49.0.3; the row is retired by the specification that moves the floor past 49.0.3 (127 may retire it in its claim list, or leave it as a fact);
- **the largest `br_table`** (3,203 targets in both binaries, well under GHSA-mghf's about 256k; EXECUTED, `../research/2026-10-24-moving-to-wasmtime-50/results/br-table-scan.txt`) is **not** a row; it is a research fact in the memo's addendum (Decision 4) with its command, the pinned wasi-sdk's `llvm-objdump -d` over both binaries with each `br_table`'s targets counted, rerun by hand at a toolchain change until the pin's floor carries mghf's fix (127). The reason: `tests/harness/wasm.py` splits function bodies and decodes only `call` and `end`, and finding a `br_table` means decoding every instruction of 89,061 bodies. A walker that does it must know every opcode's immediates (the block types, `br_table`'s vector, the memory arguments, `call_indirect`'s pair, `select`'s types, the `0xFC` prefix's bulk-memory and table forms, the `0xFD` prefix's SIMD forms with their lane indices and 16-byte constants, `try_table`'s catch list), each shape with a unit row on a hand-built body; that is well over 60 lines and a module of its own (INFERRED from the opcode tables), larger than the one row it serves, and the judge-only legs on three systems carry no `llvm-objdump` to shell out to. And wasmtime 50.0.0 carries mghf's fix (READ, `../research/2026-10-24-moving-to-wasmtime-50.md`), so once 127 lands the bound guards nothing the engine does not;
- **the proposals**: the existing `test_exceptions_are_the_standard_form` and `test_target_features_are_the_lines` hold the exception-handling form and the feature list, and `harness.wasm.parse` refuses a `rec`, `sub` or GC type, so no new row; the reason (GHSA-wjc8 and GHSA-hw8m need GC types) goes in their docstrings in words, without the advisory ids;
- the `exnref` liveness scan is **not** a row (it needs the same code-section decoder) and is recorded as a research fact with its script (`agents/h18-gc-exnref/artifacts/evidence/`), rerun by hand at a toolchain change until the floor passes 49.0.2.

### 3. `SECURITY.md`, the security model and the fuel page

The paragraph's "no wasmtime-py release yet carries a wasmtime past it" becomes "wasmtime-py 50.0.1, which bundles wasmtime 50.0.0, is released; the requirement has not yet moved to it". `SECURITY.md`, *Upstream advisories the runner handles*, gains a third bullet, under a heading that says it is **not** handled: "**GHSA-m63x-6p34-q65x** (medium, fixed in wasmtime 49.0.1): on wasmtime 49.0.0, the fuel cap and `fuel_used` under-count work a guest does inside a C++ call that ends in a caught exception or a `longjmp`; the compiled-in packages are built with exceptions on, and a guest reaches such a call from plain Python.
