## The problem

A session's phase table (`_sessions.py:115-128`) and its invariants (`_sessions.py:196-216`) say that `holder` is the turn's thread while a turn runs, and that a marked session is ended by whoever marks it or by a closer. Five paths break that promise, all under `Session.run_async` or at exit (READ, the memo's §2).

- `run_async` claims on the event-loop thread (`runner.py:1145`), so `holder` is that thread, while the turn runs on a worker (`_session_turn`, `_sessions.py:858`), which never rebinds it. A `close()` from a tool or a host function, which runs on the worker, finds `holder != me` at `runner.py:1205` and waits on `done` (`runner.py:1213`) for a turn that cannot return until the tool does: a deadlock. The docstring promises `Session.run`'s behaviour, which holds there alone.
- A queued turn cancelled after a concurrent `close()` moves STOPPING to MARKED (`_sessions.py:123`), and nothing ends a MARKED session: the reaper and `_end_dropped` act on OPEN alone (`_sessions.py:347`, `:395`), and `_claim` raises `SessionEnded` without ending it. The `close()` caller waits until exit; the store and the pipe reader thread stay. The path through `_submit` raising (`runner.py:1157-1160`) is the same hole.
- A closer thread that fails to start (`thread.start()` at `_sessions.py:319`, outside the lock) leaves the ending claimed by `_CLOSER` and the phase ENDING; the reaper swallows the exception (`_sessions.py:388`) and skips ENDING ever after; `close()` and exit wait without bound.
- At exit, `_stop_async_calls` cancels queued futures (`runner.py:541`) and the coroutine never resumes to `_release_claim`; `_sessions_at_exit` then waits on `done` (`_sessions.py:434`) for a turn that never ran.
- `_abandon_turn` (`_sessions.py:914-922`) leaves a session MARKED at the guest thread's end, and a dropped `Session` then gets no ending.

Two Low rows ride along: `_write_output` drops bytes without setting `dropped` (`_outputs.py:300-306`), and `process().pools` grows by one pool at every `set_defaults` resize (`runner.py:507`).

## Decisions

### 1. The thread that runs a turn is its holder

`_session_turn` rebinds `state.holder` to its own thread under `state.lock` as its first step, through a new event `TAKE_TURN` in the phase table, allowed from TURN and from STOPPING (a `close()` may have come between the claim and the start) and leaving the phase as it is. Invariant 2 then holds as written. A `close()` from the turn's home thread returns at once, as it does for `Session.run`; a `close()` from the loop thread or any other waits for the ending, as the docstring promises. Reason: the invariant is the right one, and the claim is the only place it was broken; rebinding at the start of the turn is one assignment under the lock the invariant names.

### 2. A session that is marked and held by no one gets a closer

Every path that leaves a session MARKED with no thread about to end it claims the ending for `_CLOSER` and starts a closer, under the same lock-then-start shape `_reap` uses: `_release_claim` when it moves STOPPING to MARKED, the `_submit`-raises path through it, and `_abandon_turn`. `_end_dropped` and `_reap` also act on MARKED when no ending is claimed, so a session that reaches MARKED by any path not listed here is still ended by the next tick or the next drop. Reason: the table already names the STOPPING-to-MARKED move; what was missing is the rule that MARKED is never a resting state for a session nobody holds. The abandoned turn's session ends through the same closer, with the ending deciding whether to run shutdown from the session's recorded end, as it does today for a stopped turn.

### 3. A closer that cannot start runs where it was started

`_start_closer` wraps `thread.start()`; on any exception it runs `_end_quietly` on the calling thread and returns, so the ending always runs and the phase always reaches ENDED. For the reaper this blocks its loop for at most one ending's `shutdown_s`; for `_claim(here=False)` and `_end_dropped` it blocks the caller the same way. Reason: a thread that cannot start is a host under pressure, and under pressure the one thing the runner may not do is lose an ending; a retry on the next tick would need a new state to remember, and an ending run inline needs none.

### 4. A claim whose turn never started is released by whoever cancelled it

`run_async` registers, beside the event in `process().live`, the release of its claim (a bound method of the session), and `_stop_async_calls` runs each release for a future its cancel refused to start. The coroutine's own cancel path (`runner.py:1166-1167`) keeps its release and the registration is removed when the turn starts or the await returns, so a release runs once. Reason: the claim belongs to the turn, and the turn's cancellation is the one event the coroutine cannot see at exit.

### 5. The two Low rows

`_write_output` sets the stream's `dropped` before it returns `size` on a failure, when it reached the stream. `_pool` shuts down and removes a replaced pool from `process().pools` once its futures are done, instead of keeping every pool ever made. Reason: each is one line, in a file this specification touches, and each is a truth the result or the exit hook reads.

### 6. The rows first

- `tests/unit/test_runner_sessions.py`: a `close()` from a tool during a `run_async` turn returns at once and the session ends when the turn returns (red today: a deadlock, bounded by the row's own deadline); a `close()` from the loop thread during a `run_async` turn waits for the ending and `shutdown_outcome` is set when it returns (red today: it returns at once); a queued turn cancelled after a concurrent `close()` ends the session and `close()` returns (red today); a closer whose `thread.start()` raises, through a seam on `process().observers.start_ending`, still ends the session and `close()` returns (red today); an abandoned turn's session, dropped, is ended by the reaper (red today).
- `tests/unit/test_runner_async.py`: a program that exits with a `run_async` turn still queued in a busy pool exits, with the session ended "closed" (red today, bounded by the row's deadline).

## Intent

After this specification, every session reaches ENDED by exactly one ending, whoever claimed its turn, whichever thread calls `close()`, and whether the turn ran, was cancelled before it started, was abandoned, or was still queued at exit. An embedder that runs many sessions from async code and closes them from tools or from other tasks never waits on a session that nobody will end.

## Design

- `src/wasi_python/_sessions.py`: `TAKE_TURN` in `_SESSION_TABLE` and `_HOLDER_EVENTS` (Decision 1); `_session_turn` takes it first; `_start_closer` runs the ending inline on a failed start (Decision 3); `_reap`, `_end_dropped` and `_abandon_turn` claim and start a closer for a MARKED session nobody holds (Decision 2); the invariants' docstring gains one line for MARKED.
- `src/wasi_python/runner.py`: `_release_claim` starts a closer when it lands on MARKED (Decision 2); `run_async` registers its release beside its event and `_stop_async_calls` runs it for a cancelled queued turn (Decision 4); `_pool` prunes the pools (Decision 5); `close`'s docstring says which thread waits.
- `src/wasi_python/_outputs.py`: `_write_output` sets `dropped` (Decision 5).
- `docs/how-to/sessions.md`: one paragraph, that `close()` from inside a turn's tool returns at once and the session ends as the turn returns, from anywhere else it waits for the ending.
- The news file `../changes/144-a-session-is-always-ended.md`: under Fixed, "A session is always ended: a `close()` from a tool during a `run_async` turn no longer deadlocks, a queued turn cancelled after a `close()` is ended by a closer, a closer that cannot start runs where it was started, and a turn still queued at exit releases its claim."
- The commits, in order: the rows seen failing; the holder and the closers; the exit release; the two Low rows; the docs and the news file.

**Different from the specification, each for a reason.**

1. **The deadlock is a host function's, not a tool's.** A synchronous tool runs on a thread of its own (the bounded invoke), and an asynchronous tool on the caller's event loop (READ, `_tool_host.py`'s `_Bounded` and `_ToolLoop`). Neither is the turn's thread, so a `close()` from a tool never deadlocked: from a tool's thread it waits, the tool call is answered "cancelled" once the stop is set, and the turn ends (INFERRED from the bounded invoke's wait, READ). A `host=` function and a provider run on the turn's own thread, the worker under `run_async`, and that is where `close()` waited on its own turn. The row therefore uses a `host=` function, and the CHANGELOG line and the docs paragraph say "a host function or a provider". After this change an asynchronous tool's `close()`, made on the event loop's thread, waits for the ending (Decision 1's rule for the loop thread); before, it returned at once, since the loop thread was the claimant. The orchestrator asked whether that wait is a cycle (the tool waits on the ending, the ending on the turn, the turn on the tool) and, for a synchronous tool, whether it is bounded only by the tool's timeout. Neither holds. While a tool runs, the turn's thread waits in the bounded invoke's `_wait`, which looks at the turn's stop at every tick, before and apart from the tool's timeout; the stop is set when the session's `closing` is, and `close()` sets it through the mark before it waits on `done`. So within a tick the turn's thread answers the call "cancelled" and abandons the tool, which runs on unseen. The guest is stopped, and the turn returns and runs the ending on its own thread. None of that needs the tool to return or the event loop to run: an async tool's task is asked to cancel through `call_soon_threadsafe`, and so are the caller's loop's tasks at the turn's end (READ, `_tool_host.py`'s `_Bounded._wait`, `_abandon`, `_cancel_post` and `_ToolLoop.close`). The behaviour is kept, and two rows guard it. `test_close_from_a_sync_tool_in_a_run_async_turn_ends_the_session` and `test_close_from_an_async_tool_in_a_run_async_turn_ends_the_session` each run the loop on a thread joined with a fail-safe. They assert that the tool's `close()` returns with the ending finished, that the turn ends "cancelled" and the session "closed" and "skipped", and, for the async tool, that the loop runs on after the await. Both are green on the branch (EXECUTED). On `dev`'s runner (EXECUTED, `dev`'s `src/` from `git archive` first on the path, `build/144/tools-on-dev.log`), the sync row is green too and the async row is red only on the wait: `[('closed', None)] == [('closed', 'skipped')]`, because the tool's `close()` returned before the ending. The docs paragraph says that a tool of the running turn waits for a short time.
2. **One entry point for every closer.** Decision 3 names a seam on `process().observers.start_ending`, which takes the session's state, not the thread. `_start_closer` now consults that observer itself and wraps both the observer and `thread.start()`: a failure of either runs `_end_quietly` on the calling thread. Every place that starts a closer calls it (`_reap`, `_end_dropped`, `Session._claim`, `_sessions_at_exit`, `_release_claim`, `_abandon_turn`), so the observer now also sees the starts from `_claim(here=False)` and from exit, which before bypassed it. No check set the observer on those paths (READ, grep of `tests/`).
3. **A release runs for a cancelled future, not until the turn starts.** Decision 4 removes the registration when the turn starts. The build keeps the future beside the release instead, and the exit hook runs a release only for a future that is cancelled; a cancelled future never starts and a started one cannot be cancelled, so this is the same rule, read from the future's own state, with no change to `_session_turn`'s arguments. A release is taken out of `process().live` under `live_lock`, by the exit hook or by the coroutine's own cancel, so it runs once. `process().live` becomes a mapping from each event to its future and release (or `None` for a call or a running turn), and `tests/unit/test_process.py`'s reset row gives its input in that shape.
4. **The pools are pruned in `_submit`, and one more row checks it.** There is no `_pool`; the default pool is made in `_submit`. `process().pools` becomes a mapping from each pool to the count of its futures not yet done. `_submit` counts each future under `pool_lock`; a done callback, `_pool_future_done`, puts the pool on a new queue of the process object, `pool_done`, without a lock; the next `_submit` drains that queue under `pool_lock`, and a replaced pool is let go once its count reaches zero (at once when it had none). That is more than the one line Decision 5 expected: knowing that a pool's futures are done needs a count or the executor's private state, and the build uses no private API. The first build decremented under `pool_lock` in the callback; the fast tier then failed `tests/checks/test_v1_4_public_api.py::test_the_pool_takes_the_newest_size` (`AssertionError: (3, [2, 3, 4])`, `build/144/fast-tier.log`), whose stand-in lock lands a `set_defaults` at each entry and so reads the callback's later entry as a resize. The callback also ran under the executor's own shutdown lock when the exit hook cancels, a nesting the lock order does not name. The queue removes both: the callback takes no lock of the runner's, and `_submit` takes `pool_lock` once, as before. A replaced pool whose last future finishes after the last submit stays until the next one, so the process keeps at most the pools of the resizes since its last submit, not every pool ever made. `pool_done` is a `queue.SimpleQueue`, which the fork reset already makes anew by its type; no lock is added. The specification lists seven rows; an eighth, `test_a_replaced_pool_is_dropped_once_its_futures_are_done`, checks this, as a new behaviour gets a check. It was seen failing on `dev`'s code in its first form (four pools kept where one was wanted); it was then amended to submit once more before it asserts, and to give `pool_done` a fresh queue, so on `dev`'s code it now fails at that monkeypatch. The reset row's input for `pools` follows the new shape.
5. **The bound on a row that would hang is a fail-safe wait, not pytest's timeout.** The project has no timeout plugin (READ, `pyproject.toml`). The rows use the existing pattern, a `join(60)`, `asyncio.wait(..., timeout=60)` or a child's `timeout=120`, each commented as a fail-safe and never a verdict; on the unchanged code two rows then left the test process hung at exit, which the builder's watchdog ended.
6. **A `close()` from the loop thread while the turn is still queued returns at once.** Until the turn starts its holder is the claimant, the loop thread, so invariant 2 makes that `close()` "inside the turn" and it marks the session and returns. The session is still ended: a turn that starts after it runs no guest code (its stop is set) and runs the ending on its worker; one cancelled while queued is handed to a closer (Decision 2); one queued at exit is released (Decision 4). Decision 1 is built as written; making that `close()` wait would mean claiming for no thread, which no decision asks for.
7. **Two rows of the session table's own check.** `tests/unit/test_lifecycles.py` requires a row for every member of the event enum (`test_the_session_table_is_exhaustive` and `test_the_session_table_is_live` failed on the first fast tier, `build/144/fast-tier.log`). The build adds `TAKE_TURN`'s two rows, from TURN and from STOPPING, each moving the holder from the claimant to the thread that runs the turn and leaving the phase and the wait handles as they were; they are claimed in the news file.

**The inline ending holds no lock** (the judgement call of Decision 3, READ, each call site of `_start_closer`): `_reap` calls it after its `state.lock` hold and after copying the sessions under `sessions_lock`; `_end_dropped`, `Session._claim`, `_sessions_at_exit`, `_release_claim` and `_abandon_turn` after their `state.lock` hold; `_stop_async_calls` runs its releases after `live_lock` is released; `_abandon_turn` runs as a step of an abandoned home, which the guest thread runs after its `home.lock` hold, or `finish` runs at once outside it. The ending blocks the thread that started it: the reaper's loop, for at most one ending's `shutdown_s` of guest code plus any call of the embedder's own code; the event loop under `run_async`'s `_claim(here=False)` or a cancelled turn's release, the same; exit, which then ends sessions one after another instead of side by side. `_State`'s invariants docstring says it in its new item 8, and `_start_closer`'s docstring says the caller holds no lock.

**Criterion 4, path by path.** The `run_async` holder: two rows. A queued turn cancelled after a `close()`: one row. The `_submit`-raises path: not driven by a row; it runs the same `_release_claim` as the cancelled queued turn, after a `close()` that would have to land between the claim and a failing submit, which no seam reaches without patching a private function. A closer that cannot start: one row, through the reaper; `_claim(here=False)`, `_end_dropped` and exit go through the same `_start_closer`. A turn queued at exit: one row, in a child. An abandoned turn: one row, which also drives `_reap`'s new MARKED branch with a session left MARKED by hand. `_end_dropped`'s MARKED branch is a safety net no listed path reaches after this change, since every lock hold that enters MARKED now claims the ending in the same hold (invariant 8); it is not driven by a row.
